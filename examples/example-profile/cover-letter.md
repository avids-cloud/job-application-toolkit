# Cover letter: Maya Chen

## Length and shape

Three quarters of a page. Four paragraphs: opening, career fit, evidence, closing. No bullet points. Single-spaced.

## Opening (one paragraph)

Lead with one specific reason this company. Not "I have long admired" or "I am writing to express my interest in." Instead, a recent piece of news, a specific bet they are making, a known operational dynamic, or a shared connection. Two to three sentences.

## Career fit (one paragraph)

Connect the role to the trajectory of my career. One sentence on what I have built or run so far in this space. One sentence on why this role is a natural next step. One sentence on why now. Three sentences max.

## Evidence (one or two paragraphs)

One paragraph per major requirement in the JD. Each paragraph leads with a specific achievement from my profile, with the number, then connects it to what the role asks for. No more than two paragraphs total. Each paragraph stands alone.

## Closing (one paragraph)

Short. Confident. Specific about the next step. No "I would welcome the opportunity to discuss further." No "It would be a privilege." Two sentences max.

## Phrases never to use in cover letters

- "I am writing to express my interest in"
- "It would be a privilege to"
- "I would welcome the opportunity to"
- "I am a perfect fit for"
- "I have long admired"
- "Please find attached"
- "I look forward to hearing from you"
- "Hardworking team player"
- "Driven and passionate"
- "Results-oriented" / "Results-driven"
- "Whether you're looking for X or Y"
- "I believe I would be a strong fit because"

## Salutation

If the hiring manager's name is in the JD: "Dear [First name Last name],"
If no name: "Dear hiring team,"
Never "Dear sir or madam." Never "To whom it may concern."

## Signoff

"Kind regards," for cover letters where the hiring manager is unknown.
"Yours sincerely," only when addressing a named person in a formal market (UK, NZ, AU traditional).
