# Voice: Maya Chen

## How others describe my voice

Direct, structured, dry. People say I "tell it straight without making it harsh." A former CEO described my writing as "like reading a well-written status update from someone who has done the work."

## Person

First person in profile and cover letters. Third person / omitted-subject in CV bullets ("Led the integration..." not "I led the integration..."). The CV bullet style is conventional in UK senior CVs.

## Spelling and conventions

UK English. Date format: "MMM YYYY" (e.g. "Mar 2020 to Present"). Currency: GBP by default. Numbers as figures rather than spelled out for anything above ten.

## Phrases I use (do use these)

- "I led..."
- "I built..."
- "I ran..."
- "Specifically..."
- "Track record of..."
- "Comfortable with..."
- "by sequencing X before Y"
- "with no material customer churn"

## Phrases I never use (hard prohibitions)

- "Results-oriented" / "Results-driven"
- "Spearheaded"
- "Leveraged"
- "Synergies" / "Synergy"
- "Drove" (overused)
- "Passionate about"
- "Stakeholders" where "people" or "customers" or "the board" works
- "Whether you're..." (cover-letter cliche)
- "It's worth noting..."
- "Furthermore" / "Moreover" / "In conclusion"
- "Genuinely" / "Honestly"
- "Robust" / "Holistic"

## Hard rules (absolute, no exceptions)

- Never use em dashes anywhere.
- Never invent numbers, achievements, or experience.
- Never claim something I cannot defend in interview.
- Numbers stay precise. If a number was estimated, write "approximately" or "circa", not a clean rounded number.

## Sentence patterns to avoid

- Never start a bullet with "Was responsible for..."
- Never start a bullet with "Led [abstract noun]" without a concrete object.
- Never use "transformed X into Y" patterns.
- Never use buzzword chains like "strategic, board-trusted capability."
- Never use abstract nouns like "reimagining", "repositioning", or "core enabler."
- Never end a sentence with "going forward."

## Sentence patterns I use

- Lead with the outcome, then the method ("Reduced X by Y by doing Z").
- Active voice. Specific verbs.
- Short paragraphs. Three to four sentences max.
- Numbers stay precise. Approximations carry the word "approximately" or "circa" so the reader knows.
- "By" or "through" introduces the method after the outcome.

## Voice test sentences

1. "Reduced cost-to-serve per customer by 47 percent over four years by consolidating eleven internal tools to four and restructuring the customer success org around segment ownership."
2. "I joined as the first COO at the start of a Series C raise. Operations were running on three sets of disconnected tools and the finance team was closing books 45 days late."
3. "Hired the CFO, CRO, CMO, and General Counsel over sixteen months. All four remained in role through the IPO."
