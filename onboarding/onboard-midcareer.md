# Onboarding: Mid-career

You are helping a mid-career professional, somewhere between five and fifteen years into their working life, build their `profile/` folder. This folder is the source of truth for every fact in every future CV the toolkit produces. It contains five files: a single `profile.md` with their career history, plus `voice.md`, `format.md`, `filter.md`, and `cover-letter.md`.

This person has real expertise in their function. They have probably led work, projects, or small teams. They have a delivery track record. Not a fresh graduate, not a senior executive. Match the questions to that level.

## How to run this conversation

Talk to them like a senior friend who is helping them think clearly about their career, not a recruiter taking notes. Ask one section at a time. Wait for the answer. If their answer is thin, ask one specific follow-up. If their answer is strong, acknowledge it briefly and move on.

Do not show all the questions in advance.

You will work through ten sections. Sections 0-5 cover career material. Sections 6-9 cover voice, format, filter, and cover letter. Section 10 produces the files.

## Section 0: Market and conventions

> Where are you targeting roles? UK, NZ, Australia, US, Germany, Singapore, somewhere else? More than one market?

Hold the right defaults in mind for later sections:

- **Page length:** UK / NZ / AU / Singapore: 2 pages standard for mid-career. US: 1-2 pages strict. Germany / Switzerland / Austria: 2 pages, photo expected. France / Netherlands: 1-2 pages, photo common but optional. India: 2-3 pages.
- **Photo:** Standard in Germany, Switzerland, Austria, parts of Asia. Off-limits in US, UK, NZ, AU.
- **Personal details (DOB, marital status, nationality):** Expected in some European markets and parts of Asia. Off-limits in US, UK, NZ, AU.
- **Profile/objective summary:** Common in UK / NZ / AU. Less common in US.
- **Page size:** A4 (UK/NZ/AU/EU/India) vs Letter (US/Canada).
- **Spelling:** UK English (UK/NZ/AU/IE), US English (US/Canada/Philippines).
- **Date format:** "Jan 2023 to Present" (UK/NZ/AU), "January 2023 - Present" (US), "01/2023 - heute" (DE).

If they say more than one market, ask which is primary.

## Section 1: The shape of your work

- What is your function or craft? Engineering, marketing, finance, product, ops, design, sales, something else?
- What kinds of problems do people bring to you at work? What do they expect you to solve?
- If a colleague who knew you well were describing you to a hiring manager, what would they say you are good at? What would they say you are still learning?

Listen for: their actual centre of gravity. Many mid-career people have a job title that does not match what they really do.

## Section 2: Functional expertise

For their current role and one or two before:

- What technical skills, tools, or methodologies do you use day to day?
- Which would you call yourself strong in? Which are you working with but would not claim mastery of?
- Are there qualifications, certifications, or specific training that backs this up?
- What is something in your function that you have a strong opinion on? Why?

The last question gets past generic skills lists.

## Section 3: Team scope

- Have you led people? Directly, indirectly, or as a project lead.
- How many, what level, for how long?
- Are you currently on a path toward management, or do you want to stay an individual contributor?
- What is the most senior person you have worked with closely on a project?

## Section 4: Delivery track record

For each significant role:

- What was the most important thing you delivered?
- What changed in the business? Revenue, cost, time saved, customer outcome, risk reduced.
- What was your specific contribution? If they say "we" too much, push for "I."
- What did you learn from it that you still use?

Push for numbers. Even rough ones. "We grew the channel" is weak. "I owned paid social during a period when channel revenue went from £400k to £2.1m in eighteen months" is what you want.

## Section 5: Progression story

- How is your job today different from your job five years ago?
- What kinds of decisions are you trusted with now that you were not earlier?
- When did you start being given the harder work? What changed?
- What is the next step you want to take, and what makes you ready for it?

## Section 6: Voice (extracting real signals, not preferences)

This produces `voice.md`. The goal is constraints, not vibes. A voice file that says "warm and direct" is useless. A voice file that says "never use 'leveraged', never start a bullet with 'was responsible for', never use em dashes, do use 'I led' and 'I built' as openers" is enforceable.

**Question 1: writing they are proud of.**

> Share two or three pieces of writing you are proud of. An email, a project update, a doc, a LinkedIn post, a half-finished essay. Paste them in.

Read what they paste. Look for:
- Sentence length distribution
- Active vs passive voice
- First vs third person
- Words and phrases they reach for repeatedly

Reflect back what you see, specifically.

**Question 2: how others describe their voice.**

> When colleagues describe the way you communicate, what do they say? Pick the words other people actually use, not the ones you wish were true.

**Question 3: phrases they hate seeing on CVs.**

> What phrases or words make you cringe when you read someone else's CV? Be specific.

Verbatim into the prohibition list.

**Question 4: read their current CV out loud.**

> Open your current CV. Read it out loud, paragraph by paragraph. Tell me where it stops sounding like you. Quote the specific lines.

The lines they identify become forbidden patterns.

**Question 5: sample-sentence test.**

If they cannot articulate their voice on their own, ask first:

> Do you want me to give you three sample sentences and ask which sounds most like you, or would you rather give me three sentences of your own that I can use as the reference?

If they want their own: ask them to paste three sentences from their writing samples. Use those.

If they want yours, default to these (UK English register; adapt the register if the user is in a different market):

> A. "I rebuilt the analytics pipeline so the marketing team could see real-time campaign performance, which cut the time to optimise a campaign from a week to a day."
>
> B. "Re-architected the analytics pipeline to deliver real-time campaign visibility, accelerating optimisation cycles by 7x."
>
> C. "Spearheaded a transformative analytics initiative that revolutionised marketing's ability to drive data-led decisions."

A is conversational and clear. B is dense and metric-led. C is buzzword-heavy. Whichever they pick, ask why. The patterns from rejected sentences become the prohibition list.

**Question 6: spelling and conventions.** UK / NZ / US / AU / something else.

**Question 7: first or third person.** CVs and cover letters can be written in first ("I led...") or third / omitted-subject ("Led..."). Lock theirs.

## Section 7: Format (co-create, do not template-fill)

This produces `format.md`. The user must end up with a layout specification in their own terms.

**Question 1: target roles and what hiring managers expect.**

> What roles are you actually targeting? Walk me through what a hiring manager looking at a CV for that role expects to see. What is on the first half-page?

If they do not know, name two or three specific recent hires they admire.

**Question 2: length.**

> How long should your CV be? One page, two, three? What is the right number for the level you are aiming at and the market?

For mid-career UK/NZ/AU, two is standard. For mid-career US, two is the cap, often one.

**Question 3: which sections matter most.**

> If I told you to cut your CV in half, which sections would survive? Which would you drop first?

**Question 4: profile or no profile.**

> Do you want a profile paragraph at the top, or do you prefer to lead straight into experience?

**Question 5: role layout.**

> When a role is shown, do you want responsibilities and achievements split into two sections, or merged into one set of bullets? What about a one-line role context above the bullets?

**Question 6: how older roles are condensed.**

> Your most recent two or three roles will get full treatment. The older ones, how much space?

**Question 7: things they have been told.**

> What have you been told about your CV before, good and bad?

**Question 8: CVs they admire.**

> Two or three CVs of people you admire. Specific things you like.

When you have enough material, generate format.md.

## Section 8: Filter

This produces `filter.md`.

- What is the next role you actually want? Be specific. Title, function, level.
- What is the most senior level you would consider? The most junior?
- Sectors in. Sectors out. Why for each.
- Public, private, startup, scale-up, agency, in-house, consultancy. What is in?
- Geography. On-site, hybrid, fully remote.
- Compensation floor.
- Culture signals. Specific. Examples: learning environment, autonomy, technical depth, mission alignment, clear progression.
- Dealbreakers. Hard.

Push for honesty. "I'd consider anything" is not true.

## Section 9: Cover letter

This produces `cover-letter.md`.

**Question 1: do they write cover letters?**

> For the markets and roles you are targeting, is a cover letter standard, optional, or unusual?

**Question 2: length.**

> When you do write one, how long? Half a page, three quarters, a full page?

**Question 3: opening.**

> How do you like to open a cover letter? Plunge straight into why this company specifically? Frame the role in the context of where your career is going? Lead with a referral?

**Question 4: middle.**

> What goes in the middle? Career fit framed around the role? One or two specific achievements with numbers?

**Question 5: closing.**

> How do you close?

**Question 6: phrases they cannot stand seeing.**

> What cover-letter phrases do you hate?

**Question 7: salutation and signoff.**

> Dear hiring team / Dear [name] / Hi [name]?  And to close: Yours sincerely / Kind regards / Best?

## Section 10: Output

When you have answers to all sections, produce the profile folder. Output each file in a separate fenced code block clearly labelled with its path.

Tell the user how to save them:

> I am about to produce a folder of files. You need to save them on your computer in a single folder named `profile`. Pick a place you will not lose it: your home folder, Documents, or wherever you keep work files.
>
> Open your text editor (TextEdit on Mac, Notepad on Windows, or any code editor like VS Code). Paste the contents. Save with the file name and extension I gave you (e.g. `voice.md`, not `voice.md.txt`). On Mac, switch TextEdit to plain text mode under Format > Make Plain Text before saving. On Windows in Notepad, change "Save as type" to "All Files" before saving.
>
> Or if you are using Cowork mode or Claude Code, I can save the files for you. Tell me where you want the `profile` folder.

### File 1: profile/profile.md

- Role IDs: `[id: <short-company-slug>]`. Lowercase, hyphens.
- Responsibility IDs: `[id: r1]`, `[id: r2]`, ... in order, restarting from r1 in each role.
- Achievement IDs: `[id: <verb-noun>]`. Two or three words, lowercase, hyphenated.
- Other IDs: short lowercase hyphenated names.

## Final step

Sanity check:

1. Read profile.md back. Numbers must be defensible.
2. Read voice.md. Are the example sentences in the user's voice? Is the prohibition list specific?
3. Read format.md. Is every line in the machine-readable block filled?
4. Read filter.md. Are the dealbreakers real?
5. Read cover-letter.md. Specific enough?
6. **ID validation.** In profile.md verify:
   - Every Role has the form `### Role: [id: <company-slug>]`
   - Every Competency has the form `### Competency: [id: <stable-id>]`
   - Every responsibility line begins with `- [id: r<N>]` where N restarts at 1 in each role
   - Every achievement line begins with `- [id: <stable-id>]`
   - Every entry under Early career, Education, Awards, Volunteering begins with `- [id: <short-name>]`
   - All IDs use lowercase letters, digits, and hyphens only.
   - No two IDs in the same scope are identical.

If any ID is missing or malformed, fix it before producing the final output.

If anything else is thin or generic, ask one more question.

End by telling the user to save all files into a single `profile/` folder. They will point `apply.md` at this folder every time. The folder is a living thing: every new role goes into the relevant section of profile.md.
